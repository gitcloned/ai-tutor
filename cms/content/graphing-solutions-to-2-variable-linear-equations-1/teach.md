# Teaching Plan: Generating solutions and graphing a two-variable equation
# Concept: graphing-solutions-to-2-variable-linear-equations-1
# Models: function-graph

## 1 (Establish the freedom to pick x)
Say: "For an equation like y = 2x + 1, you are free to choose ANY value for x. Once you pick x, y is determined."

Ask: "What do you get for y if you pick x = 0?"
  y = 2(0) + 1 = 1. Pair: (0, 1). Let the student write it.

Ask: "Now pick x = 2."
  y = 2(2) + 1 = 5. Pair: (2, 5). Let the student write it. Do ask for steps if student is not showing the steps

Ask: "Now you pick any x you like."
  Accept any valid result. If they pick a negative or fraction, work through it with them — it still works.

## 2 (Plot the pairs on the graph)
Open the graph in ask mode with the curve hidden for equation y = 2x + 1.

Ask the student to plot the three pairs they found. One at a time — they select the point, confirm it looks right to them.

After all three are plotted: "What do you notice about where these points are?" - as this as a MCQ (line, just points, curved line)

Let them describe it before revealing anything.

## 3 (Reveal the curve — connect dots to line)
Reveal the curve without clearing the student's points.

## 4 (Ask one last question)

First remove the graph

Ask: "Give me two pairs that satisfy  y = 3x − 2."

Let them work freely. They should choose x, compute y, and state the pair.
Correct pairs: (0, −2), (1, 1), (2, 4), (−1, −5), etc.

If both are correct: close.
If one is wrong: ask them to substitute back and check — do not correct directly.
